Brian Zhu

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. candidate in Operations Research at Columbia University, advised by Professor Agostino Capponi.

Broadly, I study the design and operations of digital platforms and financial technologies. Within this agenda, my current research interests focus on prediction markets and information aggregation, tokenization and its economic consequences, and AI-enabled financial decision-making.

My research has been presented and published at top conferences in economics, finance, operations research, and computer science. I have also collaborated with leading industry research teams on market analysis of stablecoins and decentralized exchange design.

Economics and Computation 2026 (Accepted)Working Paper

Auctioning Time to Mitigate Latency Races: Theory and Evidence from Blockchains

with A. Capponi

Latency competition in blockchains results in transaction spamming. Auctions for artificial time priority reduces this spam and raise platform revenue. We verify these results using data from Arbitrum’s Timeboost launch as a natural experiment.
Advances in Financial Technologies 2026 (Accepted)Working Paper

Uniform-Loss Automated Market Making for Prediction Markets

with C. Moallemi and D. Robinson

Prices in prediction markets converge to 0 or 1 by a fixed time, resulting in large arbitrage losses to liquidity providers under typical AMM settings. Proper design of AMM invariants and dynamic liquidity schedules can smooth this loss across both price and time.
Financial Cryptography 2025

Quantifying the Value of Revert Protection

with B. Bachu, C. Moallemi, D. Robinson, and X. Wan

Revert protection waives gas fees for failed transactions. On blockchains using priority gas auctions, this improves auction revenue, price discovery, and blockspace efficiency.
Cryptoasset Analytics Workshop 2025

What Drives Liquidity on Decentralized Exchanges? Evidence from the Uniswap Protocol

with B. Bachu, G. Liao, D. Liu, C. Moallemi, and X. Wan

Market depth on DEXs can be decomposed into capital deployment and capital efficiency channels. We measure how volatility, fees, adverse selection, gas costs, and external liquidity affect each channel.

with A. Capponi and A. Gliozzo

Communications Earth & EnvironmentVolume 3, Article 56 (2022)

Unsupervised machine learning reveals slab hydration variations from deep earthquake distributions beneath the northwest Pacific

with G. Mao, T. Ferrand, J. Li, Z. Xi, and M. Chen

Water trapped inside sinking tectonic plates can trigger earthquakes deep below Earth’s surface. Using unsupervised learning, we isolate a thin zone where pressurized water transforms and fractures minerals, offering clues to the mystery of how earthquakes can still occur under intense pressures where ordinary rock should not break.
