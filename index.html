About Me

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. candidate in Operations Research at Columbia University, advised by Professor Agostino Capponi.

I study how market microstructure, decentralized finance, and financial technology intersect, with current interests focusing on prediction markets, tokenization, and financial applications of agentic AI.

My research has been presented and published at top conferences in finance, economics, and computer science. I have also collaborated with leading industry research teams on market analysis of stablecoins and decentralized exchange design.

Selected Research

Auctioning Time to Mitigate Latency Races: Theory and Evidence from Blockchains

with A. Capponi

Latency competition in blockchains results in transaction spamming. Auctions for artificial time priority reduce redundant transactions and raise platform revenue. These results are empirically validated using Arbitrum’s Timeboost launch as a natural experiment.

Economics and Computation 2026 (Accepted)Working Paper

Uniform-Loss Automated Market Making for Prediction Markets

with C. Moallemi and D. Robinson

Prices in prediction markets converge to 0 or 1 by a fixed time, resulting in large arbitrage losses to liquidity providers under typical AMM settings. Proper design of AMM invariants and dynamic liquidity schedules can smooth this loss across both price and time.

Advances in Financial Technologies 2026 (Accepted)Working Paper

Agentic AI for Clustering, Relationship Discovery, and Semantic Trading in Prediction Markets

with A. Capponi and A. Gliozzo

We build an agentic AI pipeline that clusters prediction markets and identifies correlated or contradictory markets based on the their textual characteristics, then construct semantic-based trading strategies from AI-identified relations.

Financial Cryptography 2025

Quantifying the Value of Revert Protection

with B. Bachu, C. Moallemi, D. Robinson, and X. Wan

Revert protection eliminates gas fees paid for failed transactions. On blockchains using priority gas auctions, this improves auction revenue, price discovery, and blockspace efficiency.
Cryptoasset Analytics Workshop 2025

What Drives Liquidity on Decentralized Exchanges? Evidence from the Uniswap Protocol

with B. Bachu, G. Liao, D. Liu, C. Moallemi, and X. Wan

Market depth on DEXs can be decomposed into capital deployment and capital efficiency channels. We measure how volatility, fees, adverse selection, gas costs, and external liquidity affect each channel.

with A. Capponi and R. Jia

Passive market makers benefit from active market makers in DEXs due to the pro-rata sharing of trading fees, but can get crowded out when trading volume is insufficiently responsive to liquidity, worsening market quality.
